14 campaigns, every figure sourced

From the corner brewery to Nasdaq.

Crowdfunding isn't only for venture-backed startups. Breweries, cafés, family businesses and first-time founders have raised from their own customers and neighbors — some with checks as small as $100. Here are real campaigns of every size, with every figure linked to its source.

Main Street raises

Local businesses raising $25K–$1M from customers and neighbors, often as loans or revenue-share deals that pay investors back from sales.

Opened Oct 2022

Harlan County Beer Company

Brewery and family restaurant · Harlan, Kentucky

Raised
$240K
Investors

Wefunder

To bring a brewery to a restored 100-year-old building in Appalachian coal country, the founders offered a revenue share: 5% of gross sales paid out to investors every quarter. About half the money came from Kentuckians; the most common investment was $100.

What happened next: the raise paid for renovation, brewing and kitchen equipment, and Harlan's first brewery opened in October 2022.

You don't need a huge market. A small town with a clear story drew investors from 32 states.

Source: Wefunder case study (published by the platform)

Carol Pak capped her 2022 community round at $124K because she didn't have reviewed financials yet. It filled in four hours, about two months sooner than she expected, and it brought in angel investors with larger checks outside the platform.

What happened next: by 2023 Sool had sold more than 2.4 million cans, moving from H Mart into Whole Foods and Total Wine.

Start small and fill fast. A modest goal that sells out creates momentum and credibility for the next round.

Breakout companies

Startups that used crowdfunding on the way to IPOs, acquisitions, buybacks and unicorn valuations.

IPO · 2025

Beta Bionics

Medical devices: a "bionic pancreas" for people with type 1 diabetes

Raised
$1M
Investors
700+
Platform
Wefunder
Year

Weeks after Reg CF went live in May 2016, Beta Bionics became the first company to raise the full $1 million then allowed, from more than 700 investors. Eli Lilly had invested $5 million at the same valuation shortly before.

What happened next: after years of clinical work and venture rounds, the company listed on Nasdaq (BBNX) in January 2025, pricing an upsized $204 million IPO at $17 a share.

A crowd round can sit right alongside strategic investors. Matching Eli Lilly's valuation gave small investors the same entry price as a pharma giant.

Sources: Crowdfund Insider, MIT Technology Review, MassDevice

With about $271K in revenue the year before, Generation Genius raised the then-maximum $1.07 million in common stock, timing the campaign so new videos would be ready for teachers' fall buying season. The average check was about $1,400.

What happened next: the company later bought back its crowd investors' shares. KingsCrowd reports a 4.07x return overall, and about 10.2x for investors from that first round.

A buyback is a real exit path. Not every success needs an IPO or a sale; a profitable company can simply return investors' money with a gain.

Sources: KingsCrowd offering data, Los Angeles Business Journal, KingsCrowd exits report

Monogram raised more than $62 million before its IPO through online rounds open to everyone, starting in 2019. That included a 2022 StartEngine round that passed $16 million from more than 10,000 investors. About 20% of each new round came from people who had invested before.

What happened next: after a Reg A+ offering, shares began trading on Nasdaq (MGRM) in May 2023. In 2025, Zimmer Biomet agreed to acquire the company in a deal valued at about $177 million.

The crowd can be a long-term financing engine, not a one-off. Most of the later rounds used Reg A+, which allows far larger raises than Reg CF.

Sources: DealMaker case study, Benzinga, Crowdfund Insider, Zimmer Biomet announcement

Substack

Media: the newsletter publishing platform

Raised

Instead of only going back to venture funds, Substack invited its writers and readers to invest at a $585 million valuation. It started with a $2 million goal, raised it to $5 million within a day, and closed at about $7.8 million from 6,688 investors between April 6 and 30.

Why it's over $5M: the Reg CF cap is $5 million, but companies can run a parallel offering for accredited investors on the same platform, which is how rounds like this go higher.

Your users can be your most motivated investors. Substack's pitch was ownership in the platform its writers already depended on.

What the winners had in common

  1. Traction, not size. A taproom with regulars or a juice brand in local stores is enough. Investors back proof that customers already show up.
  2. An audience before the raise. Customers, writers, fans or patients who already cared. Even a few hundred loyal customers can fund a local raise.
  3. Investors who were also users. Substack's writers, Mercury's customers and Legion M's fans had a reason to want the company to win beyond the return.
  4. Credible outside backing. Eli Lilly, Coatue and later venture rounds gave crowd investors confidence in the price.
  5. A plan for the next step. Several used Reg CF as a first round, then moved to bigger Reg A+ offerings, venture rounds, or a listing.
  6. Transparency. Regular, honest updates kept investors engaged and coming back for follow-on rounds.

The other side of the ledger

These stories show what's possible, not what's typical. Small-business loans and revenue shares depend on the business staying open and paying, and in 2024 one local-business platform, Mainvest, shut down, leaving investors to collect from businesses directly. For equity raises, KingsCrowd's analysis of 6,375 companies that raised through Reg CF or Reg A+ since 2016 found:

Publicly known exits
77 (1.2%)
Publicly known failures
186 (2.9%)
Outcome not yet known
~96%

Most startups take 7–10 years to reach an outcome, so many of these companies are still operating, and many more failures than exits are expected over time. Some exits also return little or nothing to crowd investors. By late 2025, KingsCrowd had tracked more than 650 outcomes in total.

Sources: Crowdfunded Wealth on Mainvest, KingsCrowd 2018–2024 analysis, 2025 update
