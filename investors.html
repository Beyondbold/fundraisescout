Back startups with your eyes open.

Since 2016, anyone can invest in early-stage companies through Reg CF platforms. It's exciting, and it's risky. Here's what to understand before you put money in.

Find your investment limitHow to read a Form C

The honest version: most startups fail. When they do, crowd investors usually lose everything they put in. Shares are hard to sell for years. Only invest money you can afford to lose entirely, and spread it across many companies rather than betting on one.

How much can you invest?

Reg CF caps what non-accredited investors can put in across all crowdfunding offerings in a 12-month period. The cap is based on your annual income and net worth (excluding your primary home). Spouses can calculate jointly.

If…Your 12-month limit
Income or net worth is under $124,000The greater of $2,500 or 5% of the greater of your income or net worth
Both are $124,000 or more10% of the greater of the two, capped at $124,000
You're accreditedNo limit

Five habits of careful crowd investors

  1. Read the Form C, not just the campaign page. The campaign is marketing; the Form C is the legal disclosure.
  2. Check the valuation against reality. A $30M valuation on $200K in revenue needs a very good reason.
  3. Look at how they'll use the money. Vague "growth" or large founder salaries are a flag.
  4. Diversify. Small checks into many companies beat one big bet.
  5. Plan to hold for 5–10 years. Resale is restricted for one year, and there's rarely a market after that.
